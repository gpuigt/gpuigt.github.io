JAGUAR


Introduction

Despite many efforts during the past decades, the development of high-order Finite Volume schemes for unstructured grids remains limited by the requirement to compute several stencils, starting with a pure-centered formulation. Indeed, for a high-order polynomial approximation, the first step consists of computing the polynomial coefficients from the set of conservative variables (a step generally called deconvolution). In practice, each stencil must contain at least as many cells as there are monomials; in practice, it contains more, and the coefficients are obtained by solving a least-squares system.
An alternative approach directly considers one polynomial per cell, introducing as many degrees of freedom as the number of monomials in the cell. The stencil is now compact by nature (only one cell is required), but the standard Finite Volume formulation must be revisited. Among the possible methods, the best known is the Discontinuous Galerkin formulation, but we focus here on an alternative framework called the spectral difference method.

The spectral difference method

The Spectral Difference (SD) method assumes that the unknowns are represented by a polynomial of degree n. By the nature of hyperbolic equations, the flux divergence must be a polynomial of degree n and the flux is a kind of primitive of the flux divergence. In 1D, the flux is thus defined as a polynomial of degree n+1 for consistency. We do not go into details here; interested readers can download the Master's theses listed below.

Extensions of the spectral difference method

The Spectral Difference (SD) method is well defined for tensor-product elements (a product of 1D approximations in each direction). Extending it to elements other than quadrilaterals and hexahedra is not straightforward and required considerable effort. Accounting for chemistry and capturing shocks also required dedicated research:
  • We worked on the extension to triangles, tetrahedra, and prisms during the Ph.D. of Adèle Veilleux, leading to two papers.
  • The extension to reactive flows was initiated during the internship of Valentin Joncquières and continued during the Ph.D. of Thomas Marchal under the supervision of Bénédicte Cuenot and Hugues Deniau.
  • Discontinuities are handled through joint work between Hugues Deniau, Thomas Marchal, and myself on entropy-stable schemes.

ONERA research programs on JAGUAR or the spectral difference method

  • The internal project called JEROBOAM is dedicated to the use of advanced discontinuous spectral approaches for reactive flows.
  • The internal project called COCOTTES is dedicated to combustion in supersonic conditions.
  • The LMA2S (ONERA's Applied Math Lab) was involved in the Ph.D. thesis of Valentin Ritzenthaler on the use of the Spectral Difference method for Maxwell's equations.
