GammaO joint team between Inria and ONERA


Today, building the mesh is still the major bottleneck of industrial simulations.
  • Structured meshes were the preferred technique to align mesh lines and physics, especially in the boundary layer. However, the time needed to define the mesh can be considerable, and the procedure is not suited to very complex geometries. Of course, the mesh quality depends strongly on the capability to anticipate physics and on the way hexahedral blocks map the computational domain.
  • Unstructured grids generally contain elements with privileged directions (hexahedra and prisms) near the wall, with refinement in the direction normal to the wall. Tetrahedra are located around complex geometries or far from walls, while pyramids can be introduced to link hexahedra and tetrahedra. Hybrid grids require expertise to force the mesh generator to cope with one's constraints. The final mesh anticipates physics; any mistake requires regenerating the whole mesh.
    Reading those lines convinces us that the mesh generation process is the most difficult aspect of simulations since the accuracy and the capability to capture physics depend on it. Human expertise still plays too large a role, preventing full automation of the process.
    GammaO is a joint team between ONERA and Inria dedicated to the mesh adaptation procedure using simplices (triangles in 2D and tetrahedra in 3D). The idea is to start from a coarse grid and to converge the mesh and the solution simultaneously. To do so, it is mandatory to introduce a remesher that can adapt the mesh to physics using a sensor. One strength of GammaO lies in the expertise of its researchers not only on the remesher itself but also on the whole computational loop, including CFD, interpolation, error estimation, and adjoint problems.
    GammaO is based on strong cooperation between the teams, with the support of their institutions. Several topics are addressed.

    Mixed-Element-Volume discretization

    Anisotropic meshes require robust schemes, especially when the element anisotropy ratio can reach one million. Moreover, it is interesting to define the degrees of freedom and the sensors for mesh adaptation at the same location. In practice, both are at mesh nodes.
    The finite volume formulation requires the definition of the control volumes around mesh nodes, leading to a node-centered discretization. For convection, our robust scheme was proposed by A. Dervieux (and coauthors) under the name "V4 scheme". It uses a dedicated MUSCL reconstruction with a combination of upwind and centered gradients and a dedicated slope limiter. Diffusion terms are computed with a Finite Element approximation based on the gradients of the shape functions. Source terms require gradients at mesh nodes estimated using Clément's scheme. The full discretization is called the Mixed-Element-Volume (MEV) approach.

    Cooperation: G. Barreau (ONERA/DPHY), Y. Gorschka (Safran Tech), F. Pechereau (ONERA/DPHY), F. Tholin (ONERA/DPHY), C. Tarsia-Morisco (Inria/GammaO), F. Alauzet (Inria/GammaO), J. Vanharen (Inria/GammaO), A. Remigi (Safran Tech), B. Maugars (DAAA/CLEF), C. Content (DAAA/CLEF).

    Error estimate

    The error estimate is at the core of the process. It can be based on the solution itself, leading to feature-based adaptation, or on the solutions of the direct and adjoint problems, leading to goal-oriented adaptation. We work on both approaches.

    Cooperation: V. Golliet (ONERA/DMPE), F. Pechereau (ONERA/DPHY), F. Alauzet (Inria/GammaO), A. Remigi (Safran Tech) and PhD students from Inria.

    New applications

    One of my objectives for GammaO is to promote the technology for multiphysics (reentry, lightning arcs with ONERA/DPHY or turbomachinery with Safran Tech and ONERA/DAAA). Reentry flows had never been addressed with this approach before. Hypersonic flows are characterized by strong 3D shocks, whose location depends on inflow conditions. In addition, capturing the boundary layer, especially the wall heat flux, is the main objective since heat is transferred to the wall. Our strategy is based on simplices (triangles and tetrahedra) in the boundary layer, which goes against the common practice in the literature, where prisms or hexahedra are preferred. The strength of our method is the capability to add nodes exactly where they are needed and to coarsen the mesh elsewhere. A strong mathematical environment drives everything, and the whole theoretical background explains the quality of our results.

    Cooperation: V. Golliet (ONERA/DMPE), F. Pechereau (ONERA/DPHY), F. Alauzet (Inria/GammaO), A. Remigi (Safran Tech) and PhD students from Inria.
