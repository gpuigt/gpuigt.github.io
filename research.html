Research themes


My objective is to develop and enhance tools for industrial problems. These tools can be numerical schemes, methods, or procedures. The most important activities are summarized below.

Finite Volume discretization

Even if the Finite Volume method has been developed for more than 20 years and is routinely applied in industry, it still has some flaws. Indeed, the FV method is well known in the context of structured grids, but its use for unstructured grids still has shortcomings and needs additional effort. Switching to unstructured grids is an industrial prerequisite.
There are today two main approaches for unstructured grids:
  • The cell-centered finite volume method considers the mesh elements as control volumes. Mixed-element meshes, including polyhedra, are generally used. This approach is generally preferred in industry since it is the natural extension of the standard structured approach. Prisms are adequate for capturing the normal variation of quantities inside the boundary layer, where solutions and gradients must be estimated accurately.
  • The node-centered finite volume method builds control volumes around mesh nodes. These control volumes define a dual mesh. The main advantage of the procedure lies in mesh adaptation. The relation between dual cells and mesh nodes enables us to compute the mesh adaptation sensor "at mesh nodes".
I am working on cell-centered schemes for defining gradients and higher-order derivatives. The least-squares formulation requires an extended stencil and has a high computational cost. A reduced stencil can lead to poor conditioning. However, such schemes are mandatory for the MUSCL reconstruction or for the diffusion terms. Regarding the node-centered formulation, the goal is to extend the schemes to multi-physics, including multi-species reacting flows, aerothermodynamics, and plasmas. Ultimately, the goal is to apply the node-centered method to mesh adaptation.

Mesh adaptation for multi-physics

For steady flows, mesh adaptation is a way to converge the solution and mesh discretization together in a full computational loop. The proof of concept builds on more than 20 years of research at Inria (Gamma Team), including theoretical results and demonstrations. The GammaO team involves researchers from Gamma (Inria) and ONERA and focuses on the transfer of the mesh adaptation technology to multi-physics:
  • aerodynamics, including turbomachinery flows
  • plasmas, including lightning arcs
  • reentry, including wall heat flux on anisotropic tetrahedral grids

Cooperation: G. Barreau (ONERA/DPHY), F. Pechereau (ONERA/DPHY), F. Tholin (ONERA/DPHY), J. Vanharen (Inria), B. Maugars (DAAA/CLEF), C. Content (DAAA/CLEF), A. Loseille (Inria/Gamma), F. Alauzet (Inria/Gamma), C. Tarsia-Morisco (Inria), and engineers at Safran Tech.

High-order spectral discontinuous methods on unstructured grids

Many recent works focus on new numerical techniques based on the polynomial representation of quantities at each mesh element and on special treatment of data discontinuities at cell interfaces. Among them, the best known is the Discontinuous Galerkin method. At CERFACS, a new solver based on the Spectral Difference method was initiated, and the project is shared today between CERFACS and ONERA. This solver is called JAGUAR.
  • After significant effort devoted to High-Performance Computing (HPC), especially for LES, we are currently working on four axes: 1. the extension of the Spectral Difference method to handle multi-element meshes, 2. the development of a shock-capturing scheme, 3. the extension to multi-species reacting flows, and 4. the definition of an efficient implicit time integration technique.
    Cooperation: Many colleagues from ONERA and CERFACS. Strong cooperation with A. Veilleux (former Ph.D. student, ONERA and CERFACS), R. Fievet (former post-doc, RTRA and ONERA), H. Deniau (ONERA), A. Genot (ONERA), J-M. Senoner (ONERA), P. Seize (ONERA and DGA), J-F. Boussuge (CERFACS), G. Daviller (CERFACS), and M. Montagnac (CERFACS).

  • Another activity, carried out within the LMA2S lab, deals with transferring the spectral difference method from the Navier-Stokes equations to Maxwell's equations.
    Cooperation: many colleagues from ONERA. Joint work with X. Ferrières (ONERA), S. Pernet (ONERA), P. Cantin (INSA), and V. Ritzenthaler (Ph.D. student, ONERA and INSA).

Time integration techniques

The time integration procedure remains a bottleneck in many multi-physics simulations. For a long time, I was involved in time spectral methods (Time Spectral method and Harmonic Balance method) for periodic and aperiodic flows. Today, I focus my research on methods for RANS and unsteady solutions (LES).
  • A still active research area concerns the definition of an efficient algorithm for the linearized treatment of the implicit system of equations in the framework of multi-physics simulations. Indeed, even if the definition of a linear solver to invert the linear system arising from the time discretization of the nonlinear initial problem is well known, the standard definition of the matrix to invert relies on human expertise to neglect or model terms.
    Cooperation: P. Seize (former Ph.D. student, ONERA) and L. Matuszewski (ONERA).

  • Another active research area concerns the development of time integration techniques suited to large time steps and dedicated to high-order spectral discontinuous methods.
    Cooperation: P. Seize (former Ph.D. student, ONERA) and L. Matuszewski (ONERA).
